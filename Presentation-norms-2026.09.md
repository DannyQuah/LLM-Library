This file puts down key norms for my writings and presentations---both in general approach and in specifics having to do with my writings or presentation decks.  The intention here is not to train you but for us to build a shared understanding of the norms that I think work best.  

**The status of this document**  
This document is definitive as the controlling statement of presentation norms.  Where it conflicts with earlier presentations, this document takes precedence.  If I give explicit updates or say things that contradict what's in this document, flag for me so I can either amend the document or correct my statements.  More generally, if ambiguity arises, flag it by citing where this document contains conflicting statements.

**My writing style**
You already have a sense of my voice from my other writings that you have read or otherwise store in memory.  These are just some additional small points.
1. I always use the serial (Oxford) comma.
2. I prefer active voice although now and then the passive voice can provide contrast and effect.
3. I prefer short sentences although now and then a longer one can provide contrast and effect.
4. Now and then (but not too frequently) I will use marginally unusual phrasings (e.g., "rules-compatible mercantilism" rather than "incentive-compatible mechanism"; "flexible topology" rather than "variable geometry"). I try to make sure these are firmly based in some academic discipline---economics or mathematics or software engineering. They are to catch the reader's attention, stretch mental space, and help firm up an intellectual identity that is distinctively my own.


**My presentation-deck style**  

1. I will normally give you an outline of a lecture, speech, or presentation.  Whatever appears there in square brackets is a directive, the contents are not to appear in the presentation deck.
2. Use colorscheme, design, and fonts as much as possible from the LaTeX Metropolis Beamer Theme I used in 1774858148-Danny.Quah-Economic-Statecraft-Asias-Role-Arithmetic-deck.pdf and 1779184936-Danny.Quah-USD-dominance-Changing-World-Order-deck.pdf
3. However, use Noto Sans font to replace Roboto Condensed.
4. Make the fonts as big as possible without running right into boundaries; leave plenty of white space.
5. Put sufficient space below headers and headlines so that there is clear separation.
6. Use master-based footers. On the left, put the short deck title as ordinary master text. On the right, use PowerPoint's genuine dynamic slide-number placeholder for the current slide number, followed by ordinary master text containing “ / N”, where N is the verified final number of slides. Do NOT make “ / N” a PowerPoint footer (ftr) placeholder, because PowerPoint may suppress that placeholder in Normal/Slide Show view. Tighten the spacing between the slide number and the " / N". After generating the deck, verify the footer in rendered Normal view and in the exported PDF, not just in Slide Master view.
7. If it's appropriate---say for a teaching class but obviously not in a business- or policy-presentation---explicitly indicate where there is a break for discussion.
